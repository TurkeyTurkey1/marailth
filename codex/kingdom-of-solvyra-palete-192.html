Modified / Updated version

In the ancient chronicles of Maralith, it is written that the world was once thought complete.

The seas were charted, the lands named, and the skies above were believed to hold nothing but wind and silence. Yet in a single turning of the age, the heavens were broken by wonder.

Where emptiness had reigned, there rose Solvyra, a vast island suspended in the sky, encircled by eight lesser isles set in perfect, almost divine formation. This moment, remembered as The Ascension, was not seen as the slow shaping of land, but as a revelation—an arrival that defied the order of the known world.

Drawn by awe and restless curiosity, some of the people of Maradorn journeyed upward and set foot upon this sky-bound realm. They came not as conquerors, but as seekers, and in time they became the Solvyran people. From their first steps upon Solvyra, they understood that this was no ordinary land. It was not ancient like the continents below, but newly born, as though placed among the clouds by unseen hands. Thus, their civilization grew not from inherited legend, but from discovery, observation, and the quiet realization that they stood within something far greater than themselves.

Through study of the eight surrounding islands, the Solvyrans came to believe in the Eight

Gods, primordial beings who shaped existence itself. Each island bore a distinct and powerful biome—lands of storm, flame, growth, shadow, and more—each reflecting the nature of a single god. At the heart of every island stood a sacred temple, and within each temple rested an artifact of immense power. These were not mere relics, but living echoes of divine creation, left behind as both gift and responsibility.

From this understanding was born the Doctrine of Balance, the central truth of Solvyran belief. The eight artifacts, bound to their islands, form a perfect equilibrium that holds Solvyra aloft. Each one carries a unique power, mastered over generations by the Solvyran people, yet always treated with reverence and restraint. For it is known that the balance is absolute—should even one artifact be removed, disturbed, or misused, the harmony between the islands would shatter. The sky itself would turn against them, and Solvyra would begin its fall, collapsing from the heavens it was never meant to leave unguarded.

To the Solvyrans, knowledge is not separate from faith, but its highest form. They believe the gods did not create them to kneel in ignorance, but to rise in understanding. Every citizen pursues mastery in a Dregion, devoting themselves to disciplines such as philosophy, alchemy, and other profound studies. In their learning, they seek not only advancement, but alignment with the design of the gods. Their great airships, capable of ruling both sky and sea, are seen as proof of this purpose—creations born not in defiance of the divine, but in harmony with it.

The temples of the eight islands stand as sacred centers of both power and responsibility.

Within them, the artifacts are studied, wielded, and protected. They are neither worshipped blindly nor treated carelessly, but approached with disciplined reverence. To tamper with an artifact beyond what is permitted is considered a grave transgression, for it is not merely an act of disobedience—it is a threat to the very existence of all who dwell upon Solvyra.

Though they dwell above the world, the Solvyrans remain bound to it. They have not forgotten their origins in Maralith, nor do they turn away from it. Alliances endure, knowledge flows between sky and sea, and the people of Solvyra keep watch over the lands below as both allies and observers. They do not see themselves as rulers above the world, but as guardians of something within it—a delicate system upon which all their lives depend.

At the heart of their belief lies a truth spoken in both temple and hall:

The gods shaped the balance. The people were chosen to uphold it.

So long as the Eight remain in harmony, Solvyra shall drift eternal among the clouds. But should that harmony break, the sky will claim what was once given, and the fall of Solvyra will echo across all of Maralith as a reminder that even the greatest creations are bound by balance.

Religion

When Solvyra first appeared in the skies above Maralith during what would later be called

The Ascension, the people of the world did not respond with unity, but with debate. Some believed the floating island to be a divine gift, others argued it was a natural phenomenon yet to be understood, and many feared it as a sign of something unknown. The first expeditions sent from Maralith were not driven by faith, but by curiosity—groups of explorers, philosophers, and alchemists seeking to answer a single question: what exactly was

Solvyra?

Upon reaching the sky-bound land, these early explorers discovered the eight surrounding islands. Each one possessed a vastly different biome, contained and distinct, arranged in a formation that seemed too precise to be natural. At the center of each island stood a temple, and within each temple rested an artifact unlike anything found in Maralith. These artifacts radiated power, yet not chaos. When studied, they revealed patterns—structured, measurable, and strangely interconnected. It was here that understanding began to replace speculation.

Through years of careful observation and experimentation, the early scholars uncovered a truth that would shape all future belief. The artifacts were not independent objects, but parts of a larger system. When one was altered, even slightly, the entire structure of Solvyra reacted. Subtle shifts in alignment, changes in stability, and disturbances across the islands revealed that everything was connected. From this, the scholars reached a single, undeniable conclusion: Solvyra was sustained by a system of perfect balance.

With this realization came a new question—if the system was designed, then who had designed it? The Solvyrans did not immediately turn to worship. Instead, they studied further, comparing the biomes, the artifacts, and the distinct qualities of each island. Over time, a unified theory emerged: that each island represented a separate force, too unique to be the work of a single creator. From this idea, the concept of the Eight Gods was formed—not as figures seen or spoken to, but as logical explanations for the precision and diversity of

Solvyra’s design.

As more settlers arrived from Maralith and Solvyra developed into a true society, these discoveries were recorded, debated, and eventually formalized into what became known as the Doctrine of Balance. This doctrine established that the Eight Gods had created Solvyra and its surrounding islands, that the eight artifacts were the mechanisms that sustained its existence, and that the balance between them must never be broken. This was not enforced through fear, but through understanding, as the consequences of imbalance had already been observed and could not be denied.

Over time, knowledge itself became the foundation of faith. Unlike many traditions in

Maralith, Solvyra’s belief system did not rely on inherited stories, but on continued study. The system of Dregions was established, requiring every citizen to master advanced disciplines such as philosophy, alchemy, and other fields of knowledge. Learning was no longer simply a pursuit of progress—it became a responsibility. To understand the world was to honor the gods who created it.

Despite their position in the sky, the Solvyran people never separated themselves from

Maralith. They maintained strong alliances with the world below, continuing trade, communication, and shared knowledge. While some in Maralith viewed Solvyra with awe or suspicion, the Solvyrans saw themselves as part of the same world, entrusted with a unique role. They had come from Maralith, and they remained connected to it, even as they lived above it.

In the present age, the Solvyran religion stands as both belief and truth. The Eight Gods are accepted as the creators of Solvyra, and the artifacts are understood as the foundation of its existence. Their technology continues to advance, and their knowledge surpasses that of all other peoples, yet beneath all of this remains a single, unchanging understanding. Solvyra does not float by chance—it is sustained by balance. And should that balance ever fail, the fall of the island would not only end their civilization, but prove that even the greatest creations are bound by the forces that hold them together.
