Book XXIX · Redwick

Redwick and Aluven

Aluven was settled by Redwickians. In Orbon 921 the survivors of the Ranchious expedition came ashore on the western continent, lost half their number to the Dongolons, and scattered into the settlements that became Keldra, Wanna, Nandarra and Maldona. For a thousand Orbons afterwards the two nations shared a language, a history and an origin, and disagreed about almost everything else.

The rivalry was built out of ordinary things: border disputes, tariffs, fishing grounds, naval incidents, and the endless argument over who had the better claim to the settlements of the western coast. It became a war when the Aluvenian authorities executed a large number of Redwickian citizens and then murdered the diplomats sent to negotiate about it. Redwick's response was so fast that Aluvenian planners concluded their intelligence had simply failed; in fact the crown had known within days.

The Aluven–Redwick War taught both nations something they did not forget. Aluven learned that a war across the sea would not stay a distant affair on the frontier. Redwick learned that it could move an army further and faster than any rival, and that this was worth more than any fortress.

After the Great Diverse War the settlement redrew the map in Redwick's favour. Hawker, the traditional capital of Aluven, passed to Redwickian control, as did Nandarra and Maldona, and the Sangarian region was incorporated after the Great Redwick War. Redwick ended the age holding more territory than at any point in its history, and having fired very few of the shots that won it.

The result is a nation that is simultaneously the leader of the eastern alliance, the occupier of three of Aluven's most important cities, and the oldest friend of Maradorn. All three facts are true at once, and Redwickian statesmen have spent the years since explaining each of them separately to whoever was listening.
