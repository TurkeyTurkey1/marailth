Book XXI · Aluven

Hawker

Overview

Hawker is the modern capital of Aluven and serves as the political heart of the nation. Situated on the western coast of Drymoria, the city is home to the Royal Palace, the offices of King Bartholomew, and the central institutions of the Aluvenian government. Although Hawker is not the largest city in Aluven, it is regarded as the most politically important, as the decisions made within its government halls influence the entire kingdom.

The city is known for its wide boulevards, grand stone buildings and imposing civic architecture, reflecting Aluven's long history as one of the major powers of Drymoria. Citizens, merchants, soldiers, scholars and diplomats travel from across the country to Hawker seeking opportunity, influence and involvement in national affairs.

Despite being the capital of Aluven, Hawker is also one of the most politically unusual cities in the country because, following the Great War, Hawker came under Redwickian control. This created an unusual situation in which the traditional capital of Aluven remained a centre of Aluvenian identity and government while being heavily influenced by the power of its historic rival.

Geography and City Layout

Hawker is located on the western coast of Aluven, giving the city access to important maritime routes across the surrounding seas. Its position allowed Hawker to grow from a regional settlement into a major administrative and political centre.

The city is more formally designed than many of Aluven's older settlements. While cities such as Nandarra developed organically around ancient ports and trading districts, Hawker's growth was increasingly shaped by government planning. Wide roads connect important districts, while major stone buildings dominate the central areas of the city.

At the heart of Hawker lies the Government District, containing the Royal Palace, administrative buildings, military offices and the chambers used by the Aluvenian leadership. The city also contains large residential districts, merchant quarters, markets, barracks and coastal facilities.

The architecture of Hawker is intended to demonstrate stability and power. Grand stone walls, tall civic buildings and broad public spaces create a very different atmosphere from the crowded trading streets of Nandarra or the smaller, more practical settlements of the Aluvenian interior.
