Book XXIX · Redwick

Redwick

Redwick is the oldest human nation in the known world, and the only one whose founding is remembered as a landing rather than a conquest. In Orbon 1, two hundred people fled the hellish islands of the Dark Lands aboard broken boats. After almost two Orbons at sea, one hundred and fifty-three of them came ashore in what is now Fungladom. A century and a half later their descendants, led by King Orlious, reached the land that would carry the name Redwick, and from there the human race spread out across the plains of Dravale and the cherry groves of Blushwood.

Redwick occupies the eastern continent, the same great island that holds Blushwood, Karhiem, Fungladom and Corvium. Its heartland is the Plains of Dravale, wide and open and given over to grain, horses and roads; its oldest wealth is Blushwood, whose orchards and merchant houses have funded more Redwickian ventures than any crown. The capital is Redwick City, built where the Dravale road meets the sea.

For most of its history Redwick has been a naval power first and everything else second. Its influence does not come from the size of its army but from the number of ships it can put to sea, the speed with which it can move an army across the Great Sea, and the fact that the sea lanes of the known world have been patrolled by Redwickian hulls for three thousand Orbons.

  • the fleet, and the ship-holding houses that fund it;
  • the grain and horse country of the Plains of Dravale;
  • the orchards, dyes and merchant banks of Blushwood;
  • Lywra, the city of magic, and its guild of wizards;
  • the old, unofficial alliance with Maradorn, sworn in Orbon 1004 and never formally broken.

The crown governs from Redwick City, but its authority has always rested on two bargains: with the houses that build and crew the fleet, and with the guilds that pay for it. A Redwickian monarch who loses the confidence of the dockyards does not last long, whatever the law says. At the Council of Maralithian Nations Redwick is represented by Marcus Aurelin.

The nation's foreign policy can be stated in a sentence: keep the sea open. Every Redwickian war has been, at bottom, a war about whether someone else would be allowed to close a strait, a harbour or a trade route. It is a policy that has made Redwick rich, made it many enemies, and made it the natural leader of the eastern alliance.
