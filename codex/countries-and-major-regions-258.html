Book XX · Drymoria

Countries and Major Regions

Aluven

Aluven is one of the oldest and most historically important human civilisations on Drymoria.

Its origins can be traced directly to the survivors of the Redwickian expedition aboard The Ranchious. After the disastrous encounter with the Dongolons, the surviving humans escaped and established several separate settlements. These eventually developed into the major Aluvenian cities of Keldra, Wanna, Nandarra and Maldona. Hawker was founded later and eventually became the capital.

Modern Aluven is one of the continent's major powers and has played a central role in several of the greatest conflicts in Maralithian history. Its relationship with Redwick is particularly significant, with rivalry and hostility between the two nations forming the basis for numerous historical wars.

Aluven's major cities each developed their own specialisation:

  • Nandarra became a major coastal and trading city.
  • Keldra developed as another important trade centre.
  • Wanna became a smaller inland settlement located in the central dry country.
  • Maldona became strategically important because of its position near Sangar and the Ashwood Frontier.
  • Hawker, founded later than the other settlements, eventually became the capital.

Drymoria cannot be understood without Aluven. The country was the first major human power established on the continent and became the political and economic centre from which many later events developed.

Dunaria

Dunaria is one of the major nations of Drymoria and represents one of the continent's important resource-producing regions.

During the early period of human exploration, Dunaria remained unknown to the first explorers of the continent. By the end of the Aperion Era, much of Aluven was known, Sangar had been identified but not explored, and Dunaria remained beyond the understood frontier.

In the modern era, Dunaria is particularly important because of its natural resources and trade. Salt from Dunaria is exported internationally, including to Maradorn.

Shadowfen

Shadowfen is one of Drymoria's darker and more mysterious regions.

Located beyond the Ashwood Frontier, Shadowfen historically represented the edge of known civilisation for the early inhabitants of Aluven. The borderlands surrounding it were difficult to explore and became associated with exile, wilderness and danger.

Shadowfen played a particularly important role in the story of King Laperous I.

After being convicted and facing execution by the governing body of Maldona, Laperous fled along the border between the desert and Shadowfen. Expecting eventually to reach the sea, he instead continued through the wild regions of the continent for three Orbons before eventually reaching the coast of what is now Ebonwood.

Shadowfen therefore became an important part of the route that led to the creation of one of Drymoria's most historically significant independent settlements.

The region's identity is strongly connected to the unknown. Even after much of Drymoria became settled, Shadowfen retained a reputation as a difficult and dangerous place, separated culturally and geographically from the major cities of Aluven.

Ebonwood

Ebonwood is one of the most historically distinctive regions of Drymoria.

Its modern identity can be traced strongly to Laperous I, an Aluvenian outcast who fled Maldona after being convicted and sentenced to death.

After years of travel, Laperous reached the coast of modern Ebonwood and founded the settlement of Laperion in Orbon 1084.

At first, Laperous was alone.

Eventually, however, he encountered three other humans who had also been exiled or forced to flee their previous societies. Together, they returned to Laperion and began building a settlement.

Laperion quickly became known as a place where outcasts, fugitives and people rejected by other societies could begin again.

By Orbon 1100, the settlement had reached a population of approximately fifty people, and by Orbon 1130 Laperous had become its official leader.

The growth of Laperion marked the beginning of what later became known as the Laperous Era.

Laperous himself died in Orbon 1167 after being brutally attacked by a Pagaer, a large grey bear-like creature known for its vicious nature.

Despite his death, the society he created continued to grow.

Eventually, the region became associated with Blackborough, a major settlement whose population had reached approximately one thousand by Orbon 1400 through natural growth and the arrival of further outcasts.

The original settlement of Laperion, however, did not survive permanently.

In Orbon 1431, Maldonan forces attacked and ransacked the settlement. The population retreated, abandoned the town and increasingly concentrated around Blackborough and smaller settlements. Laperion was left behind and eventually became a ghost town within the forest.

Even in the modern era, Laperion remains one of Drymoria's most important historical ruins.
