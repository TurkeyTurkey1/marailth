The Gods

Beyond the four Primordials, Primordialism includes a wider family of gods and goddesses.

These gods are generally believed to represent more specific aspects of life and are descended from the Primordials.

Lucious — God of Luck

Lucious is the God of:

  • luck;
  • good decisions;
  • fortunate outcomes;
  • hindsight.

He is the son of Vindr and the twin brother of Rivian.

People may seek Lucious's blessing before making important decisions or taking chances.

Rivian — God of Risk

Rivian is the God of:

  • risk;
  • unlucky decisions;
  • bad decisions;
  • consequences;
  • hindsight.

He is the son of Vindr and twin brother of Lucious.

Lucious and Rivian represent the two possible outcomes of decision-making: fortune and misfortune.

Sothar — God of Light

Sothar is the God of:

  • light;
  • the sun;
  • honour;
  • victory.

He is the son of Birstang.

Sothar is often associated with triumph achieved honourably.

Nythera — Goddess of Darkness

Nythera is the Goddess of:

  • darkness;
  • night.

She is the daughter of Adurna.

Nythera is not necessarily considered evil. In Primordialism, darkness represents the unknown and the natural balance against light.

Morvane — God of Death

Morvane is the God of:

  • death;
  • the underworld;
  • peaceful passing;
  • ancestors;
  • ghosts.

He is the son of Adurna.

Morvane is generally associated with peaceful death rather than fear. He represents the transition between life and whatever comes after it.

Elyssia — Goddess of Love

Elyssia is the Goddess of:

  • romantic love;
  • beauty.

She is the daughter of Deloi.

Elyssia represents romantic love rather than family or brotherly love, which is more closely associated with Deloi.

Drakthor — God of War

Drakthor is the God of:

  • war;
  • strategy;
  • tactics.

He is the son of Birstang.

Unlike Birstang, who represents courage and determination more broadly, Drakthor represents the organised and strategic nature of conflict.

Velcor — God of Trade

Velcor is the God of:

  • merchants;
  • trade;
  • work.

He is the son of Deloi.

Velcor is particularly important in cities such as Point of Eloke and other major trading centres.

Aethoron — God of Wisdom

Aethoron is the God of:

  • wisdom;
  • knowledge.

He is the son of Vindr.

While Vindr represents the wider concepts of intelligence and wisdom, Aethoron is particularly associated with the pursuit and accumulation of knowledge.

Scholars and teachers may honour both Vindr and Aethoron.

Forgrim — God of Crafts

Forgrim is the God of:

  • smithing;
  • blacksmithing;
  • woodworking;
  • metalworking;
  • crafts.

He is the son of Birstang.

Forgrim is particularly important among builders, blacksmiths and craftspeople.

Marovar — God of Sailors

Marovar is the God of:

  • sailors;
  • boats;
  • rough seas.

He is the son of Adurna.

Sailors may honour both Adurna and Marovar before difficult voyages.

Sulira — Goddess of Hunting

Sulira is the Goddess of:

  • hunting;
  • beasts;
  • large creatures.

She is the daughter of Deloi.

Hunters and those working with large or dangerous creatures may seek her protection.

Lyrenna — Goddess of Joy

Lyrenna is the Goddess of:

  • joy;
  • happiness;
  • parties;
  • music;
  • celebrations.

She is the daughter of Vindr.

Festivals and celebrations often include references to Lyrenna.

Dormir — God of Sleep

Dormir is the God of:

  • sleep;
  • dreams;
  • visions.

He is the son of Vindr.

Dreams are sometimes viewed as potential sources of meaning, particularly when they occur during important periods of a person's life.

Kaerila — Goddess of Sport

Kaerila is the Goddess of:

  • sport;
  • victory;
  • competition.

She is the daughter of Birstang.

Athletes and competitors may honour Kaerila alongside Birstang, particularly before major competitions.
