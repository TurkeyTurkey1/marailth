Book XXI · Aluven

History

The Landing of the Ranchious

The human history of Aluven began in Orbon 921, during the Aperion Era.

Redwick had already begun expanding beyond its original lands and had constructed a large battleship known as The Ranchious. The purpose of the expedition was to investigate and settle the suspected lands across the Great Sea. The journey was extremely difficult, with dangerous weather, poor navigation and unfamiliar waters making the crossing far more dangerous than originally expected.

Eventually, the Ranchious reached the modern islands and coastline of Aluven.

The Redwickian settlers initially attempted to establish themselves on the land, but they soon encountered the Dongolons, the native inhabitants of parts of the region.

Dongolons are small, monkey-like creatures with six legs. They are capable of using tools and are particularly associated with spears, which they traditionally use for hunting, defence and combat. The Redwickians underestimated the creatures and treated the new land with little understanding of its existing inhabitants.

This quickly led to conflict.

The Dongolons attacked the Redwickian settlers, killing approximately 50% of the original expedition. Around one hundred humans were killed, while the surviving settlers escaped and began separating into smaller groups.

These survivors eventually established the settlements that would become:

  • Nandarra
  • Keldra
  • Wanna
  • Maldona

These cities became the foundation of human civilisation in Aluven.

The Early Aluvenian Settlements

For centuries, the settlements remained relatively separate from one another. Their geography and economic roles caused them to develop differently.

Nandarra, located on the ocean, became the major centre of trade and maritime activity.

Keldra developed into another major trading city and eventually became one of Nandarra's greatest economic rivals.

Wanna, located further inland, developed a very different character from the coastal cities. Smaller and more isolated, it became closely connected with farming, travel and life in the harsh Aluvenian interior.

Maldona, positioned near the frontier toward Sangar and the Ashwood regions, developed a stronger defensive and political role.

Over time, the population of the settlements increased through migration, natural population growth and continued contact with the rest of the known world.

The development of these cities eventually created a distinct Aluvenian identity, increasingly separate from the Redwickian civilisation from which the original settlers had come.
