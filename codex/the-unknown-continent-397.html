The Unknown Continent

The Unknown Continent is a vast landmass located far beyond the known lands of Maralith.

It is separated from the known world by an enormous expanse of ocean, making it almost completely inaccessible without advanced ships or aircraft. For thousands of years, the continent remained unknown to the people of Maralith, with no reliable maps or records proving that it existed. This changes when 10,000 Orbons come around, when the CMN launches a revolutionary rocket carrying world leaders and other important figures into the sky. During the journey, the passengers discover a massive landmass that is not connected to any known continent. The discovery immediately creates enormous interest throughout

Maralith, as the leaders realise that an entire unexplored continent has been sitting beyond the limits of their known world.

The first images of the continent show an enormous landmass covered in forests, mountains, rivers and large areas of unexplored wilderness. From the distance, there appear to be no major cities or obvious signs of civilisation. Because the continent appears largely untouched, many of the leaders begin imagining what could exist there. Some believe that it could contain enormous amounts of natural resources, while others are interested in its wildlife, geography, history and potential inhabitants. The discovery quickly becomes one of the most important scientific events in Maralithian history.

After the discovery, the nations of Maralith begin working together to determine how they could reach the continent. Scientists, engineers, architects and other experts are brought together to calculate the distance, determine the best routes and design vessels capable of making the journey. Among these young minds are five boys from Maradorn, Aluven,

Redwick, Solvyra and Frosthaven. Although they come from completely different nations, the five boys become close friends during the CMN meeting. They are fascinated by the discovery and eventually become involved in the enormous project to reach the new continent.

The first major exploration attempt does not happen immediately. In 10,002 Orbons,

Maradorn launches a smaller scout mission to gather information about the continent and the enormous ocean separating it from Maralith. The mission provides valuable information about the distance and conditions of the journey, allowing the nations to begin preparing for a much larger expedition. By 10,003 Orbons, preparations for the major expedition are complete.

The expedition consists of approximately 10,000 men and women. Around 2,000 are scientists, engineers, explorers and other specialists, while approximately 8,000 are soldiers. The military presence is relatively small compared with the size of the expedition because the leaders do not expect to encounter a major threat. They believe that the continent is mostly unexplored wilderness and that any inhabitants they encounter will probably have little reason to oppose them.

Almost all of the major world leaders decide to join the expedition. One of the few people who questions this decision is Maxorian, who believes that sending so many leaders away from Maralith could be dangerous. However, the world is currently experiencing a period of peace and stability, and the other leaders believe that the opportunity to explore an entirely new continent is too important to miss. They therefore leave their nations behind and begin the journey across the ocean.

The continent itself is extremely difficult to explore. The coastline is enormous, with rocky cliffs, beaches, natural harbours and dense vegetation stretching along the shore. The expedition eventually establishes a large temporary camp near a suitable harbour where ships can unload supplies, repair equipment and prepare for exploration further inland. At first, the expedition believes that this location could eventually become the foundation of a permanent settlement.

As the explorers move inland, they discover that the continent is far more difficult to navigate than expected. Huge forests cover large areas of the land, while rivers, mountains and dense vegetation make travel slow and dangerous. The largest region they encounter becomes known as the Great Jungle. The jungle stretches deep into the continent and contains enormous trees and thick vegetation that make it almost impossible to see far ahead. The deeper the expedition travels, the more isolated they become from the coast.

The Great Jungle later becomes extremely important to the five boys. Somewhere within this enormous region is believed to be the Elemental Temple, a mysterious location that has never been properly mapped. There are also stories surrounding the Mythalis, who may possess knowledge or abilities capable of helping the people of Maralith. The boys eventually decide that finding the Mythalis and reaching the Elemental Temple may be their only chance of stopping the destruction spreading across their homeland.

The expedition gradually begins discovering signs that the continent is not uninhabited.

Small settlements begin appearing throughout the regions they explore. These settlements contain people who are physically similar to humans but have their own cultures, traditions, technologies and understanding of the world. The inhabitants are initially extremely cautious of the newcomers because they have their own ancient beliefs and taboos surrounding the continent.

The native people are particularly fearful of certain areas of the interior. They warn outsiders not to travel too far into the continent and speak of places that should not be entered. The expedition initially struggles to understand these warnings. Many of the Maralithian explorers assume that the stories are simply myths or superstition. However, the people who live on the continent have passed these warnings down through generations, meaning that their fear has a much deeper history than the expedition initially realises.

Eventually, the expedition discovers a small human-like settlement and sends a diplomatic team into the town. Instead of attacking the visitors, the inhabitants welcome them with surprising hospitality. They provide food, water and other supplies to the expedition, allowing the explorers to restock after their long journey. The encounter completely changes the expedition's understanding of the continent because they realise that its inhabitants are not automatically hostile.

The people of the settlement also possess knowledge and technology that the Maralithians have never encountered before. As a gesture of friendship, they provide the expedition with several objects, including technology and magical items. These gifts demonstrate that the civilisation of the Unknown Continent is not primitive as some of the Maralithians had originally assumed. Instead, its people have developed their own methods of combining knowledge, magic and technology.

The expedition eventually discovers that the small settlement is not an isolated community. It is part of a much larger civilisation spread across the continent. The Maralithians simply have not explored far enough to understand its true scale. Roads, settlements, ruins and other structures begin revealing that intelligent civilisation has existed on the continent for a very long time.

This creates a major difference between what the Maralithians expected to find and what actually exists. They arrived believing that they had discovered an empty continent waiting to be explored, mapped and potentially claimed. Instead, they have entered a world that already has its own people, history, beliefs, technology and mysteries.

The continent also contains enormous mountain ranges further inland. These mountains create natural barriers between different regions and make exploration even more difficult.

Some of the native people consider certain mountains sacred or dangerous, although the expedition does not initially understand why. Rivers flow down from the mountains and cross the jungle before eventually reaching the coast, creating natural routes through the continent that the explorers can use to move further inland.

Meanwhile, the five boys are travelling through the continent under completely different circumstances. They are no longer simply young engineers fascinated by the discovery. The war in Maralith has changed everything, and they realise that their homeland is in serious danger. With limited resources and little assistance available, they begin travelling through the jungle in search of the Mythalis and the Elemental Temple.

Their journey is far more difficult than the expedition's exploration. The expedition has thousands of people, ships, supplies and equipment, while the boys have very limited resources and are travelling through unfamiliar territory. They must rely on information provided by people they meet along the way, old stories and whatever knowledge they can gather about the interior.

Eventually, the boys learn that the Elemental Temple is real and that it may be connected to the mysterious forces of the continent. They also learn that the Mythalis may possess the knowledge or power necessary to help stop the war consuming Maralith. Their journey therefore becomes a race against time. Every day they spend searching for the temple is another day in which the war continues without them.

At the same time, the expedition is running low on supplies. After travelling so far from

Maralith, maintaining food, water, fuel and equipment for 10,000 people becomes increasingly difficult. The discovery of the settlement therefore comes at a crucial moment.

The hospitality of the native people gives the expedition the resources it needs to continue its exploration.

Just as the expedition is preparing to continue its journey, something unexpected happens.

A small aircraft appears in the distance. The aircraft is one of the advanced Solvyrian designs capable of travelling enormous distances using a combination of battery, solar and engine technology. It has been sent from Maralith carrying an urgent message.

The message informs the expedition of the situation that has developed while the leaders have been away. The peaceful world they left behind no longer exists. The political situation has collapsed, Aluven has declared war on Maradorn, and the conflict has spread across the known world.

The expedition is suddenly faced with an impossible decision. They travelled across the ocean believing that they were beginning one of the greatest exploration missions in history.

They have discovered an entirely new continent and encountered a civilisation they never knew existed. Yet while they were gone, their own world descended into war.

The Unknown Continent therefore becomes more than simply a new location in the story. It becomes the place where the Maralithians discover that their understanding of the world is incomplete. What began as an expedition to discover new land eventually becomes a search for knowledge, friendship and potentially the means to save their original world.

The greatest mystery of the continent remains the interior. The deeper regions have not been properly mapped, the Elemental Temple remains undiscovered, and the true nature of the Mythalis is still unknown. The native people know far more about these places than the

Maralithians do, but they are reluctant to reveal everything they know because of the ancient taboos surrounding them.

For the people of Maralith, the discovery of the Unknown Continent changes their understanding of the world forever. They had believed that they understood their planet and its peoples, but the continent proves that enormous parts of their world remain unknown.

The expedition has only explored a fraction of it, and beyond the settlements, mountains and jungle lies a much larger mystery waiting to be uncovered.

Council of Independent Settlements Dwarf village - Durnak

Nestled among the rugged hills of the Unknown Continent lies Durnak, a small and peaceful village inhabited by a community of dwarves who live entirely upon the surface. The settlement was founded generations ago after its inhabitants were cast out by Avarenth, the capital of the Unknown Continent, following a dispute between the dwarves and the ruling authorities. Forced to leave the cities and settlements they once called home, the dwarves travelled into the mountains and established Durnak among the rocky foothills, building sturdy stone homes, workshops and communal halls across the landscape. Despite their exile, the people of Durnak refused to become bitter or hostile, instead creating a village founded upon hospitality, cooperation and acceptance. Their doors remain open to travellers of every race, and visitors are often greeted with food, stories and a warm place beside the fire. It is during their travels towards the Unknown Continent that the leaders of Maralith unexpectedly stumble upon Durnak, initially unaware of the village's troubled history with Avarenth. The dwarves welcome the Maralith delegation without hesitation, forming an unlikely friendship that eventually develops into an important relationship between Durnak and Maralith. While Avarenth continues to regard the villagers as outcasts, the leaders of Maralith come to see Durnak as something quite different: a small community that transformed rejection into belonging and proved that a people do not need a capital, a throne or great wealth to have a home.

Newford

Newford is a small but lively town situated at the foot of a vast mountain range on the Unknown Continent, where towering sandstone peaks, rugged cliffs and dense forests rise dramatically behind the settlement. Originally founded beside an important crossing point at the edge of the mountains, Newford gradually became a popular stopping place for travellers, explorers and merchants journeying through the region. Despite its relatively small size, the town is renowned for its strong community and the remarkable people who have made it their home. Among its most prominent residents is Gavy, owner of the NC Newsagency Corporation, whose network of newsagencies stretches throughout almost every town on the Unknown Continent and eventually expands into Maralith following the establishment of alliances between the continents. Wayne, one of Newford's most recognisable residents, serves as a local bus driver, carrying people between the town, surrounding settlements and the mountain communities. Newford's rugged surroundings have also shaped its culture, with hiking, climbing and mountain exploration forming an important part of local life. The town is particularly famous for its creamy peas, a simple but beloved local dish that has become an unofficial symbol of Newford. With the mountains towering above it and new people continually arriving through its streets, Newford has become a place defined not by its size, but by the people, stories and memories that have gathered there.
