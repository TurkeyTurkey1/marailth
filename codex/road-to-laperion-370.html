Road To Laperion

The bell above Pearcy General Store rang softly, but no one entered through the door. Dust floated in Blackborough's stagnant air, caught through the thin light shafts slipping through the front windows.

Hank Pearcy leaned on the counter, watching the empty shelves his grandfather once proudly stocked. Business had not improved in weeks, most days the only sound was the creak of old wooden floorboards, and the odd sigh from Stan, Hank's best friend, who was sorting through forgotten boxes in the shop's back.

"We're running out of time," Stan muttered.

Hank didn't reply. He already knew.

Later that afternoon, as the quiet of the shop settled again, Stan continued to sort through neglected crates while Hank moved toward the back shelf which had not been touched in many years, wearing dust like a second skin. Behind a stack of faded ledgers, his hand brushed something unexpected.

Grabbing hold of the object, Hank withdrew a worn leather-bound book, its surface aged and softened over time. Embossed upon its cover was the unmistakable imprint of the Pearcy family crest, a broken crown atop wolf's head. Hank opened it carefully. Within were densely written notes, fragmented cartographic sketches and repeated references to a place known only in myth; Laperion. Said to be the original town of Ebonwood, toppled many years prior during Aluven's brutal reign. Within his grandfather's unmistakable sharp angular handwriting, stood a concealed identity. Before he could process the implications, the front bell rang sharply. Stan entered from the counter area; a sealed envelope held tightly in his hand.

"Hank... it's from the bank," he said, his voice tense. "They're shutting the store, unless the debt is compensated this week". Hank's eyes moved between the letter and the book. For the first time, the store's collapse no longer felt like the only thing at stake. That night, under flickering candlelight, Hank and Stan read in silence. The book spoke of hidden paths, an ancient sealed city and an entrance protected by the Guardian trial. Laperion wasn't lost, nor myth, but concealed.

At dawn, they left Blackborough.

Ebonwood's forest swallowed them quickly, tall trees bending overhead like watching figures. The air grew colder and thicker the deeper they travelled, and the world behind them faced into memory. The map in their hands led them forward, but not without challenge, by the third day exhaustion pressed down on them. Yet Hank and Stan never turned back, and at the edge of the deepest valley in the Ebonwood forest, they finally found it, a structure of ancient stone, buried in roots, dirt and time. Hank looked at the book and then the structure, "This is the place". The two of them stood there, feeling the ground breaking disruption deep in the earth, as the Guardian of Laperion rose. Massive and silent, it rose like a shadow shaped by centuries of conflict. Its armour fused with living stone and eyes glowing with ancient judgement. Its presence bent the air around them, its voice echoing through the valley, "Only those worthy of the Pearcy line may enter what was once sealed".

The trial began, and the Guardian stuck without hesitation, testing them, breaking rhythm, finding cracks, forcing them apart. Stan was thrown back against the ancient stone, and Hank struggled to stand as every attack seemed impossible to counter. Then Hank understood, the Guardian wasn't testing strength or power but rather their resolve. Hank stood his ground, even when injured and afraid. Stan seeing this stood his ground battered and bruised in the same way. Together they refused to retreat not as warriors but as people who would not abandon the truth that they had uncovered. The Guardian paused, silence fell and slowly the Guardian lowered its weapons and stepped aside.

The entrance to Laperion unlocked.

Beyond wasn't ruin, but survival, a hidden city preserved beneath time itself, towers still standing and dim lights still glowing. Laperion radiated an energy, locked in time, waiting for centuries. Laperion had not fallen, it was protected and concealed. As Hank stepped forward, the stone beneath his feat reacted. Ancient markings flared to life around him, responding to his presence. And in that moment, the truth revealed itself completely: the Pearcy line was not accidental, but descended directly from King Laperious I, founder of Laperion. Barison had not been a simple shopkeeper; he had been the last guardian, sealing the city to protect it after its destruction at the hands of Aluven. And now, the role was passed on. Hank stood in silence as the weight of it settled. The Guardian knelt. The city waited. But Hank did not feel like a king or chosen heir, rather he felt like a boy from a failing shop in Blackborough.

Later, at the threshold between worlds, Hank made his choice. He did not seal Laperion, nor claim it. Instead, with Stan beside him, he reopened Laperion's connection to the world beyond. The hidden city would no longer remain forgotten. Together, they returned; not to rebuild the old store, but to begin something new. A new store, a place between worlds, where the legacy of Laperion and Blackborough could finally meet. And above its door, the Pearcy name remained; not as a memory of decline but as the beginning of something greater.

The bell rang once more. And this time, someone stepped inside.

Marius Thornvale and Oakley

Far from the kingdoms of Maradorn and Aluven, nestled within the dry woodlands of the Ashwood Frontier, lived a forgotten explorer named Marius Thornvale. Years ago, Marius had been commissioned by Maradorn to map the uncharted coastline west of Aluven and the wild borderlands near Shadowfen. As his expedition sailed along the rugged coast bordering the Ashwood Frontier, a powerful storm swept in from the sea, driving his vessel onto hidden rocks and scattering his crew. Marius survived the wreck and spent weeks following the coastline in search of his companions, but found no trace of them. Eventually, he discovered a freshwater spring flowing from the hills and followed it inland to a small river winding through the frontier’s dry forests. Seeing fertile soil, fresh water, and shelter beneath a towering oak tree, he decided to settle there and built a humble cabin from timber and stone. During his first winter alone, he rescued an orphaned bear cub trapped beneath a fallen tree and named him Oakley. As the years passed, the pair became inseparable companions. Travellers occasionally spoke of a mysterious woodsman and a great bear roaming the frontier, but few ever saw them. Hidden among the trees, Marius and Oakley lived a peaceful life, tending their garden, exploring ancient ruins, and watching over the wild lands that had once left them both alone.

Rimaco is a major military city located on the northernmost tip of the island of Corvium, directly beside the Floripian Sea. Although it is officially part of Corvium and ultimately falls under the rule of the Corvium Emperor, Rimaco possesses a highly distinct identity within the empire. The city serves as Corvium’s principal military stronghold and one of its most important docking and naval destinations, with approximately 650,000 military personnel associated with Rimaco. Many of these soldiers are stationed throughout Corvium or deployed elsewhere rather than permanently living within the city itself. Rimaco’s armed forces operate with their own traditions, hierarchy and sense of identity, while remaining loyal to the Emperor and fighting alongside the wider Corvian military when required. Its society is therefore heavily influenced by military rank and service, making Rimaco feel almost like a military nation within Corvium, despite remaining firmly under imperial rule.
