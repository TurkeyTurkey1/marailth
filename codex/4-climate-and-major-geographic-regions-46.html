4. Climate and Major Geographic Regions

The Continent of Maradorn contains several dramatically different environments.

The Jungles of Maradorn

The jungles are among the defining natural features of the continent. Dense vegetation, intense humidity and dangerous wildlife make many areas difficult to navigate.

These jungles were responsible for the deaths of many of the earliest human explorers and remain closely connected to the history of the Mythali.

Large areas remain sparsely inhabited compared with the major settlements. The wilderness contains important natural resources, ancient sites and ecosystems that have shaped Maradorian culture for thousands of Orbons.

The Plains of Untail

The Plains of Untail provide one of the continent's largest open environments. Their terrain contrasts strongly with the surrounding jungle and mountainous regions.

The plains historically allowed easier movement, settlement and communication. Their accessibility was one of the reasons the first human explorers were able to establish themselves there after arriving by sea.

Untail remains an important region for travel and continental connection. It is also a major historical landscape, with its name permanently associated with the Treaty of Untail.

Coastal Regions

The continent possesses significant coastlines that connect it with the wider known world.

Point of Eloke is particularly important among these regions, acting as a connection between inland communities and international maritime routes.

The coastline was also critical to early exploration. Human contact with the continent began through maritime travel, and the surviving Redwickian explorers were eventually escorted back to the coast after their encounter with the Mythali.

Modern coastal settlements continue to support trade, travel and diplomacy.

Mountain and Elevated Regions

Maradorn also contains important mountainous and elevated landscapes. These regions form natural barriers and contribute to the geographical diversity of the continent.

Mountains and elevated areas have influenced settlement patterns, travel routes and the location of important landmarks and institutions.

Stoomes and the Strategic Territory

The geography surrounding Stoomes has particular strategic value because of its role as Solvyra's continental military base.

Its location allows Solvyra to operate beyond its floating island and maintain a direct connection with the southern continent.
