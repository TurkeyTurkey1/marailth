Two faiths, one world

The Pantheon

Four Primordials made the world. Fifteen gods govern its smaller concerns. And above it all, eight artifacts hold an island in the sky.

The making

Before the world existed

Primordial belief does not begin with a throne or a first king. It begins with four forces and a moment of rage.

According to Primordial belief, before the world existed there were the four Primordials: Birstang, Adurna, Deloi and Vindr. Together they created the lands, oceans, skies and underworld. Deloi shaped the land, Adurna filled the world with water, Vindr formed the skies and air, and Birstang provided fire and heat.

The creation was not entirely peaceful. Birstang became enraged, and during that rage created Pyrothas — a hellish region fundamentally different from the rest of Maralith, associated with the destructive side of fire. Fire itself is not considered evil. It is courage, determination and the willingness to fight. Pyrothas is what happens when those forces lose balance.

Beyond the four Primordials, Primordialism includes a wider family of gods and goddesses. They represent more specific aspects of life and are descended from the Primordials. The fifteen below are the named children; their domains are the small, daily things people actually pray about — luck, risk, trade, sleep, sport and the sea.

The first four

The Primordials

Fire, water, earth and air. Every other god in Maradorn descends from one of them.

Fire · he / him

Birstang

Courage, fighting spirit, determination, competition, physical strength and sport. His animal is the dragon. His rage made Pyrothas. Father of Sothar, Drakthor, Forgrim and Kaerila.

Water · she / her

Adurna

Defence against evil, mystery, the unknown, protection from dark lands and dark people, the oceans and waterways. Mother of Nythera, Morvane and Marovar.

Earth · she / her

Deloi

Family, life, caring, generosity, the land and living creatures. Her animal is the dog, most commonly a golden Labrador. Mother of Elyssia, Velcor and Sulira.

Air · he / him

Vindr

Intelligence, wisdom, thought, knowledge — and cricket. Father of Lucious, Rivian, Aethoron, Lyrenna and Dormir. The most cerebral of the four, and the only one with a favourite sport.

The wider family

The Fifteen Gods

The children of the Primordials. Smaller domains, closer to daily life: a safe voyage, a good decision, a fair price, a full night's sleep.

Lucious

Luck · son of Vindr

Fortune, good decisions and fortunate outcomes. Twin of Rivian. People seek his blessing before chances that cannot be taken twice.

Risk · son of Vindr

Risk, unlucky decisions, consequences and hindsight. Twin of Lucious. Between them they hold both possible ends of every choice.

Light · son of Birstang

Light, the sun, honour and victory — particularly triumph that was earned honourably.

Darkness · daughter of Adurna

Darkness and night. Not evil: in Primordialism darkness is the unknown, and the natural balance against light.

M

Death · son of Adurna

Death, the underworld, peaceful passing, ancestors and ghosts. Feared less than he is trusted: he is the transition, not the punishment.

E

Love · daughter of Deloi

Romantic love and beauty. Family love belongs to Deloi; Elyssia holds the other kind.

D

Drakthor

War · son of Birstang

War, strategy and tactics. Where Birstang is courage in the broadest sense, Drakthor is conflict organised into a plan.

V

Velcor

Trade · son of Deloi

Merchants, trade and work. Particularly honoured in Point of Eloke and the great trading cities.

A

Aethoron

Wisdom · son of Vindr

Wisdom and knowledge — the pursuit and accumulation of learning. Scholars honour Vindr and Aethoron together.

F

Forgrim

Crafts · son of Birstang

Smithing, blacksmithing, woodworking, metalworking — every craft that turns raw material into something that lasts.

M

Marovar

Sailors · son of Adurna

Sailors, boats and rough seas. A difficult voyage may be given to both Adurna and Marovar.

S

Sulira

Hunting · daughter of Deloi

Hunting, beasts and large creatures. Hunters and those who work with dangerous animals seek her protection.

L

Lyrenna

Joy · daughter of Vindr

Joy, happiness, parties, music and celebrations. Festivals rarely forget her.

D

Dormir

Sleep · son of Vindr

Sleep, dreams and visions. A dream at the right moment in a life may be read as more than a dream.

K

Kaerila

Sport · daughter of Birstang

Sport, competition and victory. Athletes honour her alongside Birstang before a major contest.

Above the world

The Eight of Solvyra

Solvyra keeps a different faith: eight gods, eight islands, eight artifacts. Their balance is not a metaphor. It is the mechanism that keeps the island in the sky.

The Solvyrans came from Maradorn, but their civilisation grew around the eight islands that appeared with Solvyra. Each island bears a distinct biome — stone, library, forest, volcano, storm, deep rock, old forest, shadow — and each holds a temple and an artifact.

The Doctrine of Balance is absolute. Should even one artifact be removed, disturbed or misused, the harmony between the islands would break. The sky would turn against them, and Solvyra would begin its fall.

“The gods shaped the Balance. The people were chosen to uphold it.” The Doctrine of Balance

No one of the Eight is considered more important than another. Arvena provides strength; Morak knowledge; Sicora life; Drenos energy; Calen change; Velkar stability; Sirael memory; Nyrex protection. Remove one and the system is incomplete.

The artifacts are not relics to be admired. They are the physical components of the system that allows the civilisation to exist — studied, wielded and protected, but never treated carelessly. To tamper with one beyond what is permitted is not merely disobedience. It is a threat to everyone who lives above the world.
